0.6.0 — 2026-09-02

Tethered shooting, per-photo edit history with undo, look profiles, a RAW denoiser, windowed big-library scans, and a top-to-bottom visual refresh — everything that has landed while release publishing was paused.

Tethered shooting (Sony first)

  • Plug in a camera and shoot. With a Sony body in PC Remote mode (validated live on an a7C II), every shot appears in culler moments after the shutter: the JPEG shows the instant it lands and the RAW attaches behind it, so the screen never waits on a 47 MB file, and bursts drain at full wire speed. Downloads retry on their own and the app stays awake for the whole session. Any other camera gets the same tethered ingest; remote control is Sony-only for now, and culler says so honestly.
  • Remote shutter: Shift+Space fires the camera from culler — from the grid too.
  • Live view (B): the camera's live feed takes the photo's place on the cull screen (about 25 fps over USB). A fresh capture holds briefly for review, and Shift+Space fires straight through it.
  • Hold focus (L): park the half-press once and every following shot fires instantly at that focus — for catching the exact moment.
  • Timer: intervalometer, self-timer and bulb exposures from one rail button, with a live countdown in the corner readout.
  • Camera control from the info panel: aperture, shutter speed, ISO (with Auto), exposure compensation and white balance as − / + steppers, plus manual-focus nudge keys [ / ] (Shift for coarse, Cmd for fine). Rapid steps queue smoothly — no waiting between clicks.
  • Camera vitals: battery, drive mode and shots remaining in the corner readout and the info panel — polled gently, always yielding to photo transfer.
  • Session folders in the sources pane. Tethered shots land in a dated folder per day, and the camera row expands into past sessions: continue one, rename, delete (two-click confirm) or start a fresh one — browsable even with no camera plugged in.
  • Live mirror: every shot can back up automatically as it lands, to a folder or straight to an Immich server, with retries and a loud warning if the destination goes away — a dead destination never holds up newer shots.
  • USB link speed in the info panel ("USB 2.0 · 480 Mb/s"), with a nudge when a faster port would help — a slow hub is the usual reason transfers crawl.

Develop

  • Edit history and snapshots, per photo. ⌘Z / ⌘⇧Z undo and redo everything — ratings, flags, develop, crop. A History card in the develop panel lists every step: click one to restore that state (non-destructively — the restore is itself undoable), or promote it to a named snapshot; a Snapshots card keeps named states to return to. All of it persists in the sidecar and survives restarts.
  • Look profiles replace the Match JPEG button. The develop panel's look picker applies a complete camera-style look — tone, color and per-hue adjustments — with the exposure matched per shot. Built-in: Vivid (Sony); drop your own profiles in ~/.culler/looks/. The picture style the camera recorded in the photo is recognized and pre-highlighted (never applied on its own), and Auto (Shift+A) on a RAW applies it — one key per photo gives a whole set the camera's own look.
  • Color Mix: eight per-hue bands of hue / saturation / luminance — as sliders or a draggable curve — for the color work a global saturation slider can't do. Saved to the sidecar like everything else.
  • RAW noise reduction. Developing a RAW now applies denoising — ISO-aware on Auto, adjustable via the new Denoise slider (double-click returns to Auto). Dark, high-ISO shots stop looking grainier than the camera's JPEG, and exports carry it too.
  • A redesigned develop panel: grouped cards, rebuilt sliders with finer damped drags, scroll-to-nudge (the panel no longer scrolls underneath), click-the-value-to-type, double-click-to-reset, and values that light up when off default.
  • White-balance eyedropper. "Pick gray point" in the Color card (or press Q): click something neutral in the photo (a gray card works great) and Temp/Tint are set so it renders neutral — real Kelvin on a RAW, slider offsets on a JPEG. Clipped spots are refused with a clear message, and Esc cancels.
  • Match Look no longer fits automatically. Entering M freezes the set and changes nothing; the fit runs when you press the new Match button in the panel, and Reset all returns every photo to how it was at entry — so a fit you don't like is one click to inspect and one click to back out.
  • Match a scattered series. Select photos from anywhere in the library, Shift+Y to show just the selection, M — Match Look now works over exactly that set (a progression series, not just a burst), with an honest count when a selection exceeds the 24-photo window.
  • Matched crops. C now works inside Match Look: crop the reference to set the series' frame, then each other photo opens in an align view — the reference stays put, solid, while your photo rides on top as a faint overlay that you drag, scroll-to-size and straighten until the subjects line up. \ previews the result solid. Reset means "back to the reference's placement", and — like the rest of the session — nothing is written until M commits.
  • Re-rendering from inside a stack updates the cover on exit. G into a stack, develop, J, G out used to keep showing the old version until you switched albums — the collapsed cover now swaps to the fresh upload immediately.
  • Crops show on already-uploaded edits immediately. A new crop on a photo whose display is culler's own uploaded render used to stay invisible everywhere until the next J — committing a matched series then looked like nothing happened. It now shows at once in every view (scrolling, grid, previews), and J bakes it into the server copy as before.
  • The crop controls are a side pane now. They used to float over the top of the photo — and over the landmark panes — as a card; cropping now docks its controls on the right like the develop panel, so nothing ever covers the image while you frame it.
  • Landmark align. When poses shift too much for hand-dragging, align by matching points instead: Shift+C (or the Landmarks chip) in the align view opens a three-pane layout — the reference, your photo, and the live result. Click 2–8 distinctive points on the reference (an ankle, the nose, a hip ridge), click the same numbered points on your photo, and from the second point the crop solves itself — position, size and tilt at once. Tab skips a point that's out of view (any two shared points are enough), a magnifier follows the cursor for precision, ⌫ undoes, right-click (or the hover ×) removes a specific point, and Enter accepts and moves to the next photo. Nothing is written until M commits, as ever.
  • Landmark-view captions sit under their photos. Each pane's caption used to anchor to the bottom of the screen, where it collided with the status line into overlapping text; captions now ride directly beneath the photo they describe.
  • Match Look sessions now write history. Committing with M records one entry per changed photo ("Match look" / "Matched crop"), so Cmd/Ctrl+Z can step a committed session back per photo.
  • Matched preview. K inside Match Look shows the whole set as a grid with its live tone and crop — the series exactly as it will commit, tracking every slider move; click a photo to focus it.
  • The eyedropper works inside Match Look. Q on the focused photo neutralizes its white balance — the per-photo color step a months-apart series needs before the tone fit. Samples stay with the session: M commits them, Esc unwinds them.
  • The crop window slides past the frame. When a tighter-framed photo needs its subject placed beyond its own edge — matching a series often does — keep dragging: the outside renders and exports as clean white padding, in the editor, the preview and the file alike.
  • The align view has a legend. A caption under the photo names which is which (solid: <reference> · faint: <this photo>), both while aligning and in the result preview.
  • Full controls in the align view. Corner handles on the frame drag-scale your photo, ↑/↓ size it from the keyboard, Cmd/Ctrl+arrows nudge its position (Shift = coarser steps everywhere), and a quiet outline shows where your photo's actual edges are.
  • Fixed: the double apply after saving a JPEG-only photo. On a photo without a RAW file, J's render replaced the display and the crop, rotation and tone were then applied again on top — a tighter, re-rotated picture on screen, and a second J made it worse. Baked renders now paint plain everywhere (main view, filmstrip, grids, Compare), and a re-J re-uploads the same picture.

Immich

  • Your edits and history now live on the server. On a remote photo, J saves the develop settings, crop, full edit history and snapshots with the photo on the server, alongside the rendered JPEG it already uploads — reload the library or open it from another machine and everything is back where you left it. Edits you haven't committed with J stay local to the session.
  • Big libraries open in windows. Past roughly 5,000 photos, a library no longer loads whole: the newest couple of thousand open immediately and older months load as you approach them. Markers show where the gaps are (click one to load it), Shift+S opens a date picker that jumps straight to any month, and the sources row offers "Load all" with a live tally.
  • Scans recover instead of failing. Failed pages retry with backoff on their own, the partial-scan warning gained a one-click Retry, and scanning is gentler on modest home servers.
  • Both current Immich generations are supported. culler discovers the server's API version and reports a compatibility verdict when you add or open a server. On v3 servers, archived photos stay out of the timeline and culler's edits keep their identity.
  • The info panel (I): camera, lens, file size, place and description for every photo on every source. On Immich it grows server powers: edit the description in place, Favorite (the same axis as your Pick — starring works both ways), the people the server recognized, and the photo's albums.
  • Album picker (Shift+I): sort photos into server albums without leaving the cull — type to filter, Space toggles membership.
  • Locked folder (v3 servers): move a photo into the server's locked folder from the info panel. It's one-way from culler — only Immich's own web app can bring it back, and the panel says so.
  • Favorites and flags on unrated photos now stick. Newer servers reject the "no stars" value culler used to send, so every favorite or flag write on an unrated photo failed and silently retried forever. culler now says "no rating" the way the server expects.
  • No more black previews after J. The uploaded render becomes the photo's preview immediately instead of racing the server's thumbnail job, and a preview that failed to download retries when you return to the photo.
  • Re-rendering an edit is safe. J on an already-edited photo renders from the RAW again (never from its own previous output) and keeps filenames sane instead of stacking "-edit" onto "-edit" — and when nothing actually changed, it recognizes the identical render and leaves the stack alone (that corner case used to trash the stack's own cover, hiding the whole stack from the server's timeline).
  • Same-named photos say where they live: photos with the same filename in different folders carry a quiet folder tag in the overlay, so stacking no longer looks broken.

Uploads

  • Already-stored photos skip in milliseconds. Before any bytes move, a checksum precheck asks the server what it already has — re-running a batch flies through everything that made it the first time.
  • Retry is the button. After a batch with errors the primary button becomes "Retry N failed…": one click re-runs the batch, the precheck skips the stored photos, and the summary names which photos failed and why.
  • Honest progress: the batch's total size up front, a byte-accurate bar per file, "838 MB left · 3.1 MB/s", a live speed graph, and an amber note when nothing has moved for a while.
  • Big files on slow connections no longer fail at exactly 20 seconds — a whole-request time limit was cutting them off; uploads now take the time they need.
  • Move to Immich, safely. When a batch is verified fully stored on the server, a "Trash N originals…" offer appears under the summary: arm with one click, confirm with a second — and at that moment the server re-verifies every single file once more. Anything it can't vouch for is kept, and if the server can't be reached, nothing is touched. Originals go to the system Trash.
  • Sending files to the Trash is instant — it used to route through Finder, which on some Macs sat out a two-minute timeout and failed.

Export

  • Exports now match straight-out-of-camera quality: JPEGs encode at quality 95 with full-resolution color by default (a saved setting keeps its old value).
  • The JPEG Quality slider is always reachable: it now has its own card in the batch panel on every source — it also governs J, which quietly followed it all along; the first J mentions it once.

Multi-select

  • Select several photos and act on them at once. Cmd/Ctrl+click toggles a photo in the filmstrip or Grid, Shift+click selects the run, and from the keyboard Y toggles the current photo, Shift+arrows sweep, Cmd/Ctrl+A selects the whole view and Cmd/Ctrl+D clears. Selected photos wear a blue check chip, the corner readout and Grid header count the set, the marking keys (pick/reject/stars/labels) act on all of it when you're on a selected photo, and the batch panel's Scope menu gains Selected (N) so any batch action runs on exactly those.
  • Shift+Y shows only the selected photos — cull a hand-picked shortlist with every tool, the grid included. The view tracks the selection live (deselecting drops the photo on the spot), and clearing the selection returns to All with a note.
  • Sweeping deselection: deselect a photo, and the very next Shift+click or Shift+arrows deselects the whole run — pruning a big selection is one gesture. Shift+click ranges always start at the photo you're on.

The new look

  • A top-to-bottom visual refresh. Every panel — batch, crop toolbar, sources form, timer, help — now shares one consistent dark design: uniform control sizes, type, forms and keycap chips. The help overlay is grouped by topic and finally scrolls instead of clipping, and the phantom resize cursor on the side rails is gone.

Fixes

  • The info panel's Move to locked folder action now looks like a button (hover highlight) instead of a bare text line.
  • Grid view: Shift+Space now fires the tethered shutter as the menu says (it used to peek the photo instead).
  • Shift+A auto tone on a RAW now reads the RAW histogram, matching what the panel's Auto button always did.
  • Filenames arriving from a server or camera are reduced to a safe name before writing, so a malicious or confused source can no longer write outside the chosen folder.
  • Shift+J (overwrite JPEG) now verifies its backup and writes it atomically — a stale or partial backup can no longer cost a camera original.
  • A sidecar that exists but can't be read is left untouched instead of templated over — other tools' metadata in it survives.
  • A batch finishing while Compare, Survey or Match Look was open could crash or mark the wrong photo; it now waits until you leave the view.
  • Re-adding a server you already saved updates its API key instead of creating a duplicate entry — the natural key-rotation path.
  • Grid view: clicking a photo far from the current one no longer makes the surrounding thumbnails flicker while they re-render.
  • Re-editing an already-uploaded edit (a new crop or grade, then J) no longer silently re-uploads the previous picture when the RAW can't be downloaded or decoded — the upload stops and the status line names the real cause, so the retry actually carries your edit.
  • Downloading a large RAW (for J's re-render or 1:1 zoom) now gets the same generous time budget as uploads instead of dying at 20 seconds on a slow connection.
  • Match Look's align view: the size handles now sit on your photo's outline corners instead of the reference frame — the reference carries no drag controls.

0.5.0 — 2026-08-02

culler now tells you when there's a new culler — plus a batch of fixes and polish straight from the same live-server field session.

  • Update notice. On launch, culler makes a single anonymous HTTPS request to GitHub for a small version file (at most once a day — no account, telemetry, or usage data). When a newer version exists, a quiet ● update vX.Y.Z note joins the corner readout and an "Update available" item joins the Help menu; clicking either opens the release notes in your browser. Nothing pops up, nothing installs itself, and if you're offline the check fails silently. Add disabled=1 to ~/.culler/update.conf to turn it off.
  • Server exports now match your screen. On an Immich source, J for a RAW+JPEG pair used to render the camera JPEG with your slider values — not the RAW render the develop panel was showing — so the uploaded picture could look different from what you graded. The export now bakes the same full RAW demosaic the panel shows, and a new server round-trip test proves the uploaded file is byte-for-byte what was rendered.
  • Re-uploading a pair can't double its stack. When part of a pair is already on the server (say the RAW, from an earlier upload), a new upload now joins the existing stack — newest JPEG in front, old members kept — instead of creating a second stack over the same photo. Re-running an already-finished batch changes nothing at all.
  • "Some photos didn't load" now says why. When timeline pages fail to load from a server, the warning names the failed pages and the server's actual error instead of only a count — and each failure is also logged to the console (look for culler: scan: lines in Console.app), so a recurring problem can actually be diagnosed.
  • The manual shows real photographs. Every screenshot in the manual is reshot with real RAW+JPEG pairs instead of generated test images.

0.4.1 — 2026-08-02

Field fixes from a real holiday-culling session against a live Immich server, same day as 0.4.0 — all remote browsing:

  • Portrait photos from a server now develop and export the right way up. Server previews arrive pre-rotated, but the original files — used for full zoom, RAW develop and export — don't; culler treated both the same, so portrait RAWs developed sideways and a compensating crop then baked wrong-way exports. Orientation is now tracked separately for the preview, the original, and the RAW member of a stack, so every path decodes upright.
  • Stacks appear while the library is still loading. Server-side stacks used to snap into place only after the whole scan finished — on a big library that read as duplicate RAW+JPEG pairs for a minute. The stack relations are fetched once up front, so photos group correctly the moment they stream in.
  • Big remote libraries open newest-first and stay put. The end-of-scan swap used to reverse the order out from under you (and leave the filmstrip showing stale thumbnails).
  • Re-exporting an edit (J) replaces your previous culler edit on the server instead of piling up a new version each time — and the new render joins the albums the photo was already in, so albums keep showing it. A camera pair inside a server stack keeps its stack identity too: the re-render joins the real stack (edit + JPEG + RAW), never a partial one that would orphan the RAW.
  • If stack information can't be loaded (a key without permission, a slow server), the scan now says so in the warning pill instead of silently showing everything unstacked.
  • Fixed: the × that removes a saved source flickered away under the pointer and the click opened the source instead.

0.4.0 — 2026-08-02

culler goes public. Downloads, the manual and these release notes now live at https://mertenssander.github.io/culler-releases/ — no invitation needed. And a field session against a real Immich server shaped a batch of upload improvements:

  • Uploads show progress and can be cancelled. The batch panel shows a live "Uploading N of M" with a progress bar, plus a Cancel button that finishes the photo in flight and then stops — no half-uploaded pair, and re-running is safe (the server recognizes what's already there). While the panel is closed, the batch's row in the top-right pill carries the same ✕.
  • Pick which server an upload goes to. With several Immich servers configured, the batch panel gains a Server picker — no silent default — and album choices follow the chosen server. The completion summary names where everything went.
  • Sources have names. The Immich form takes an optional name ("Home NAS" reads better than an IP in the server picker), and R renames any saved source in place.
  • RAW+JPEG pairs stack with the JPEG as the cover on the server, matching what culling shows you.
  • "Uploaded, but…" is a warning now, not a failure. When photos land but stacking or the album add fails — commonly an API key minted before your server knew about stacks; the manual's permission table covers it — the batch says so honestly instead of calling uploaded photos failed.
  • culler now runs on Intel Macs: releases are universal binaries.

0.3.0 — 2026-08-02

Buttons that teach their keys. A commissioned design exploration became culler's new edge chrome — every mouse control now shows the keyboard shortcut it stands for, then gets out of your way.

  • The rail (left edge): the current mode's verbs as keycap buttons — Pick, Reject, Clear, Rate, Grid, Compare, Filter while culling. Hovering teaches; the Filter and Rate buttons open real palettes (click a filter to jump straight to it; click a star chip). Buttons you've used about five times politely dim — the rail retires itself as you learn. ‹‹ collapses it to a thin line; rest the pointer on the line to bring it back.
  • The spine tabs (right edge): CULL · DEVELOP · CROP · BATCH name the workflow. Click to switch; rest on a tab and a palette fires that mode's one-shot actions (auto tone, reset…) — from anywhere.
  • The develop panel grew up: Auto / Reset / Match JPEG as a full-width row under the title, Copy / Paste / Before-After at the foot, keycap chips on everything, and the sliders scroll on small windows instead of clipping.
  • Overviews got stronger: Survey gained the Grid's peek (Space — a real loupe with zoom, winnowing keeps working), both overviews now accept filter changes in place (the grid re-points live, Survey re-deals), every mode hop works uniformly from both (E C V M S, plus K⇄N between them), and A reaches the batch panel from each. Persistent styled key-hint strips replace the old status-line text that any action would overwrite.
  • New filter: Picks + unrated — everything except your rejects, in the F cycle right after Picks and in the Filter menu.
  • The old bottom action dock is retired — everything it did lives on in the rail, the spines, and the ⋯ menu (now beside the rail, styled like the palettes).
  • Fixed: the develop pane could slowly widen until it covered the window.

0.2.1 — 2026-07-30

  • Server-side ratings now show up. Stars set in Immich's own web app (or by any other tool) were silently invisible when browsing a server: real servers report the rating in a different place than culler read.
  • Archive now works against a real server. The batch Archive action used a legacy API field that current Immich accepts and then ignores — archived photos now actually leave the timeline.
  • The Immich form teaches the API key: where to create one (Account Settings → API Keys) is written right under the field, and the manual gained a permission table for locked-down keys.
  • Under the hood: every release is now built by a pipeline that must first pass a live integration suite against a real Immich server — the two fixes above are what its first run caught.

0.2.0 — 2026-07-30

  • Batch from the grid: press A inside the Grid view (K) to open the Batch panel without leaving the grid — decide what happens to your picks and rejects while the whole set is in view. Marking keys keep working beside the open panel; A closes it again.
  • Menu bar knows the grid: while the grid is open, menu items that don't apply are greyed out (previously a menu click could stack Compare or a filter change on top of the grid). Batch… and Auto-advance stay enabled — their keys work in the grid.
  • Clearer scan progress: the top-right pill now says "Scanning photos" while a server timeline streams in. That count is culler reading the server's catalog — image bytes are only ever fetched around where you're looking, never the whole library.
  • Lower memory in long grid scrolls (thumbnail cache tuned down).
  • Manual: installation now leads with the DMG; the Immich chapter explains what the scan does and doesn't download.

0.1.0 — 2026-07-29

First packaged release — a native macOS app for extremely fast, keyboard-driven culling of RAW+JPEG photo pairs, with develop baked in.

  • JPEG-first culling: flags, stars, color labels, filters — everything writes to standard XMP sidecars, originals untouched.
  • Develop on the RAW: opening the develop panel demosaics the RAW (Sony in-camera lens correction included); white balance in real Kelvin, tone/curve/vignette, Match JPEG one-click fit.
  • Multi-photo tools: Compare (V), Survey (N), Match Look (M — grade one photo, the whole burst follows), and the Grid view (K — live overview with a progress tally, Space peeks any photo full-size).
  • Sources: local folders, SD cards, network shares, and Immich servers (browse, cull with live sync, upload/download with stacking).
  • Mouse path for new users: action dock, ▾ menu, clickable stars, edge navigation — all invisible until the mouse moves.
  • Native menu bar, JPEG export with full EXIF carry-over, batch copy/move/trash/upload.
