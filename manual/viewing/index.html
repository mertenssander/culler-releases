Viewing and comparing

Zoom

  • Z, Space, or double-click toggle between fit and 100%. The zoom lands on the point under your pointer; with the pointer elsewhere, it centers on the camera's autofocus point when the EXIF carries one.
  • Scroll or pinch over the photo zooms continuously (up to 8×), anchored at the pointer. Shrinking back to the fit scale snaps to fit.
  • Drag to pan while zoomed. Full resolution decodes lazily on zoom — the view responds instantly (briefly soft) and sharpens when the full decode lands.

Before / after: \

\ toggles the untouched original — no tone, no crop, no rotation — and tags the overlay [before]. Press again for your edit.

Grid view: K

Zooming all the way out: K spreads the whole working set (the current filter) across a scrolling grid, with a live tally at the top — photos, picked, rejected, undecided, and the selection count while one exists — so one glance answers "where are we?". Every cell wears the filmstrip's badges (flag dot, stars, color bar, and the blue check chip on selected photos), rejected photos dim so the undecided work pops forward, and develop edits show in the cells. Cmd/Ctrl+click and Shift+click build a selection here exactly as in the filmstrip.

Space on the cursored cell peeks it full-size — a real loupe render, not a bigger thumbnail, so Z/scroll/pinch zoom and drag pans (the overlay says [peek]). Space, Esc, Enter, or K pops back to the grid exactly where you left it; arrow keys keep flipping through neighbors without leaving the peek, and marking keys work throughout.

The grid is live but steady: badges and edits update the instant you mark, but a photo whose new flag would drop it from the active filter stays on screen until you exit — nothing is ever yanked out from under your cursor. An explicit filter change is different — that's you asking for a reshuffle: F, Cmd+1–5, and the rail's Filter palette work inside, and the grid re-points at the new set immediately. In Survey the same keys re-deal the survey from the new set.

Grouping the grid: G

G inside the grid splits it into groups, each under its own header row: by day (one group per capture date — TUE 29 SEP 2026 · 214 PHOTOS), then by moment (MOMENT · 8 PHOTOS · 14:32:05, with the single photos between two moments gathered into a quieter SINGLES group), then back to the plain grid. Photos without a capture time gather under UNDATED. Grouping only changes the layout — nothing is filtered, moved or saved, and it stays as you left it until you quit.

Each header counts its group's picks, rejects and undecided photos as you mark them, and carries:

  • a select box on the left — click it to select the whole group (empty box: none selected; a dash: some; a check: all). Click a fully selected group's box to deselect it. The group's photos join or leave your selection like any other.
  • the title — click it to move the cursor to the group's first photo.
  • on a moment, a Survey button — it leaves the grid and opens Survey on exactly that moment, the same as pressing N on one of its photos.

The groups follow the grid's current view: under a filter or inside a pivot they group just the photos you're looking at, and a group keeps its photos while you mark (the grid's steady membership), even when a mark would move them out of the filter. The arrow keys walk the rows as you see them — up and down step over the headers and land in the same column, or in the last photo of a shorter row — and PgUp / PgDn move a screenful of rows.

Compare view: V

Pick the best of two head to head. V pins the current photo as the Select (left — your benchmark) and puts the next photo beside it as the Candidate.

KeyAction
← →walk the Candidate through the current filtered view
↑promote: the Candidate becomes the new benchmark, the next photo challenges
↓swap the two sides
Tab (or click a pane)move the focus ring — rating/flag/label keys hit the focused pane
Z / double-clickzoom both panes together, anchored at the same relative point

Judging never auto-advances mid-comparison, and a photo whose new flag would drop it from the active filter stays on screen until you exit — the comparison is never yanked out from under you. Walking a moment is four keys per photo: →, look, ↑ if it wins, repeat.

Moments

culler groups photos of the same situation on its own — a moment: photos from one camera, each taken within 4 seconds of the one before it. That's a burst of fast frames, or a slower run of the same situation — the same pose shot a few times over while you wait for the smile. A photo taken longer after the previous one starts something new. Photos a different camera took in between never join, and neither do photos without a capture time. It's read from the capture time the camera wrote (to the millisecond where the camera records it) — nothing is stored, and your marks never regroup anything. A long steady run — a timelapse at one frame a second — is one long moment; Survey deals it 24 photos at a time.

The gap is a setting: Max gap between photos in Settings (Cmd+,), from 0.5 to 10 seconds in half-second steps. The filmstrip regroups as you drag it, and two lines under the slider count the moments in your library and name the one you're on. A longer gap only merges moments, it never reshuffles them. Set it to 0.5 s to group only strict bursts — frames fired in quick succession. (It's stored as moment_gap_ms in ~/.culler/settings.conf, in milliseconds.) When , or . finds no moment in the view, the status line says the gap in effect and that Cmd+, changes it.

  • The filmstrip draws a thin bracket under each moment's thumbnails. The bright one is the moment you're on — exactly what N will open.
  • The overlay tags the photo with its place in the moment: [moment 3/8].
  • , / . jump to the first photo of the previous / next moment, skipping single photos — in the normal view, the Grid, and Survey (where they deal that moment instead). The status line counts along: moment 4 of 17 · 8 photos.

Moments follow the current filter: only the photos the view shows count, so a moment the filter cuts down to one photo is a single photo there. That makes the second pass free — under Unrated, every moment you've already judged leaves the view and . steps straight over it. Moments aren't shown inside a stack.

Survey view: N

Winnow a moment on one screen. On a photo that's part of a moment, N lays out exactly that moment — the status line says survey: 8 photos · moment at 14:32:05 — and in a moment of more than 24 photos, the 24 around your position ("24 of 61 in this moment"). Anywhere else it lays up to 24 photos around your position out in a grid. Survivors grow as losers leave.

KeyAction
arrowsmove the focus ring through the grid
Xreject and remove — the flag is written, the grid reflows, and the next photo slides into the same cell so you can keep hammering
⌫remove from the grid without judging (no flag written)
Enterkeep this one — pick the focused photo and reject every other photo here you haven't decided on yet
, / .deal the previous / next moment
1–5, P, U, 6–9mark the focused photo (never removes it)
Esc / Nexit onto the focused survivor

Enter is the one-key moment verdict: the focused photo becomes a Pick, and every other photo still in the grid that has no flag and no stars is rejected and leaves the grid. Photos you've already picked or rated stay exactly as they are — an earlier Pick is never demoted — and there's no confirmation, because every flag is one key to undo. With auto-advance on (T), the next moment is dealt straight away.

Space peeks the focused photo full-size through the real loupe — zoom with Z or scroll, flip through survivors with the arrows, keep winnowing with X — and Space/Esc pops back to the grid exactly as you left it (same gesture as the Grid view's peek).

Winnowing all the way to zero exits by itself — rejecting an entire bad moment is a legal outcome. Develop edits show in every cell. The rail keeps the marking verbs at the left edge in both Survey and Grid — they act on the focused cell, and Reject winnows just like X.

There is a third multi-photo mode: Match series (M) freezes a set and makes it belong together — every frame aligned to the photo you're standing on and, when you want it, its tone matched too. It lives with the develop tools — see Matching a series.

Compare and Survey are both unavailable inside a stack; step out first.

Info panel: I

I (or the INFO tab on the right spine) opens a right-side panel with the focused photo's details: filename, capture time, camera and lens, the exposure triangle, pixel dimensions and file size, and your own culling state. It stays put while you keep culling — the panel always describes the photo you're on — and works inside the Grid and Survey views too, where the small cells have no room for metadata.

The Captured, Camera and Lens rows — and a Moment row when the photo is part of one — are links. Each shows, on the right, how many photos share that value, with a small arrow; click the row to pivot to exactly those photos (Shift+G does the same from the keyboard, and Esc brings you back). Where a pivot can't start — Survey, crop, inside a stack — the rows read as plain text.

On an Immich source the panel adds the server's knowledge of the photo: its description, recognized people, the albums it belongs to, its favorite state, and where it was taken. People and albums come from the server on demand — the panel shows quiet placeholder bars for the moment that takes, and photos you have already visited show instantly. A local folder has none of this, and the panel simply ends after the file details — no empty rows, no placeholder text.

The Favorite row is the same thing as your Pick flag on Immich sources — picking a photo stars it on the server, and a photo starred elsewhere arrives here already Picked. One axis, two names.
